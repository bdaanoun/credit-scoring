Overfitting Prevention and Training Stopping Strategy
======================================================

To reduce the risk of overfitting, the XGBoost model uses several
regularization techniques and early stopping.

First, the maximum tree depth is limited to 5 using max_depth=5.
Limiting tree depth prevents individual trees from becoming overly
complex and memorizing the training data.

The min_child_weight parameter is set to 3. This prevents the model
from creating overly specific branches based on nodes with insufficient
information, which helps reduce model complexity.

Subsampling is also used. The parameter subsample=0.8 means that each
boosting round uses approximately 80% of the training observations.
Similarly, colsample_bytree=0.8 means that each tree uses approximately
80% of the available features. These sources of randomness help reduce
the model's dependence on specific observations or features and improve
generalization.

The learning rate is set to 0.05. A relatively small learning rate
makes each new tree contribute only a small correction to the existing
model. This allows the model to learn progressively rather than making
large changes in a single boosting round.

Early stopping is also used with early_stopping_rounds=50. During
training, ROC AUC is calculated on both the training and validation
sets after every boosting round. The validation AUC is used to monitor
generalization performance.

The learning curve shows that training AUC continues to increase as
more boosting rounds are added, while validation AUC eventually
reaches a plateau. After the best validation AUC is reached, additional
boosting rounds improve the training AUC without improving validation
AUC. This indicates that the model is beginning to overfit the training
data.

Therefore, the best iteration is selected based on the highest
validation AUC rather than the highest training AUC. Training is
stopped after 50 consecutive boosting rounds without an improvement in
validation AUC. This prevents unnecessary additional training and
limits the model from continuing to learn patterns that do not
generalize to unseen data.

The final stopping point is therefore justified by the learning curve:
the validation AUC has stopped improving, while the training AUC
continues to increase, providing evidence that further training would
primarily increase overfitting rather than improve generalization.
